Case study 01 · Remote sensing & machine learning

Adapting a segmentation model to aerial-scale land cover

MSc THESIS — "Evaluating Foundation Model Adaptation for High-Resolution Land Cover Segmentation Using RGB Aerial Imagery" — CRANFIELD UNIVERSITY, 2026

Foundation models trained on medium-resolution satellite imagery (Sentinel-2, Landsat) are transforming Earth observation — but can they be adapted to fine-grained, sub-metre aerial imagery? This thesis tested that question on 1,027 RGB aerial patches over the Peak District National Park, comparing a supervised baseline (SegFormer-B0) in both zero-shot and fine-tuned settings.

Built in PyTorch with Hugging Face Transformers, trained on Google Colab (T4/A100 GPUs). Zero-shot transfer proved negligible — confirming a substantial domain gap between satellite pretraining and aerial-scale detail. Fine-tuning with a combined Cross-Entropy + Dice loss over 11 epochs closed part of that gap, though gains concentrated in dominant land-cover classes due to class imbalance. A structured literature review across 12+ candidate foundation models (scored on scale-awareness, spectral compatibility, and HRAI suitability) informed the model selection.

Case study 02 · Applied GIS & web mapping

Turning collection priorities into an operational route map

Built during a cross-disciplinary group project for Instaveg, a fresh-produce supply chain. I led the spatial analysis: scoring 12 verified villages in Kenya on climate risk, produce quality, and collection urgency, then using OpenRouteService to calculate real truck travel times and distances between them. The result is an interactive LeafletJS map that turns that scoring model into an operational routing tool.

Role: GIS & spatial analysis leadClient: InstavegTools: ArcGIS Pro · Leaflet · OpenRouteService

Screenshot — the live map updates independently of this page.

The map plots each village colour-coded by urgency tier (high / medium / low), draws the optimised travel path between them, and surfaces a data pop-up per stop — distance, travel time, cargo weight, priority score, and an efficiency score (value collected per minute of travel). Behind the map sits a small scoring framework I designed:

  • Climate Risk Score — normalised temperature, rainfall, wind speed & elevation (ERA5 + SRTM via Google Earth Engine)
  • Priority Score — 0.5 × quality (packable %) + 0.5 × climate risk
  • Efficiency Score — weighted priority ÷ travel time, to rank collection value per minute on the road
